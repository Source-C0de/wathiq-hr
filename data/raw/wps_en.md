# Wage Protection System (WPS) — Overview

The Wage Protection System (WPS) is an electronic payroll system mandated by
the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD) of Saudi
Arabia. It is designed to ensure that all private-sector workers in the
Kingdom receive their wages on time and through approved channels.

## What WPS requires from employers

1. **Pay wages through an approved channel.** Wages must be paid through a
   Saudi-licensed bank or a money-exchange company approved by the Saudi
   Arabian Monetary Authority (SAMA). Direct cash payments are not permitted
   for WPS-covered establishments.

2. **Upload monthly salary files.** Employers must submit a salary file
   (WPS-compatible format) to the MHRSD portal before the salary payment date,
   declaring the wages due to each worker.

3. **Cover all workers.** Every worker on the employer's file — Saudi and
   non-Saudi, full-time and part-time — must be included in the monthly
   salary file.

4. **Pay on time.** Wages must be credited to the worker's bank account by
   the end of the first working day of the following month at the latest,
   matching the day declared in the salary file.

5. **Keep records.** Employers must keep payroll records and bank
   transaction references for at least two years.

## What happens if an employer fails to pay salaries through WPS

Failure to comply with WPS triggers a graduated series of penalties managed
through the MHRSD's "Nitaqat" compliance scheme and the WPS monitoring
dashboard:

1. **Warning and a grace period.** The first time an establishment misses a
   payment or fails to upload a salary file, the MHRSD issues a warning and
   gives the employer a short period (typically 10–15 days) to regularize.

2. **Block on new work permits and Iqama transactions.** If the non-compliance
   continues, the MHRSD blocks the establishment from issuing new work
   permits, renewing existing Iqamas, or transferring sponsorships. This
   effectively freezes the employer's ability to hire or retain staff.

3. **Nitaqat downgrade.** Persistent WPS non-compliance causes the
   establishment's Nitaqat band to drop (e.g. from Platinum/Green to Yellow
   or Red), which limits the establishment's access to expatriate workers
   and exposes it to further MHRSD inspections.

4. **Field inspection and on-the-spot fines.** MHRSD inspectors can visit
   the establishment to verify wage records, interview workers, and impose
   direct fines. Repeated non-compliance can result in cumulative fines
   that escalate per worker per month.

5. **Suspension of services.** In severe or persistent cases, all MHRSD
   e-services (work permit issuance, transfer of sponsorship, change of
   profession, etc.) are suspended until the employer regularizes all
   outstanding wages and uploads complete salary files for at least three
   consecutive months.

6. **Worker right to terminate and claim.** A worker who is unpaid for two
   or more consecutive months is entitled under Saudi Labour Law to treat
   the contract as terminated by the employer, leave the Kingdom without
   the employer's consent, and claim all outstanding wages and end-of-service
   benefits through the labour courts.

In short, the WPS makes wage non-compliance a self-reinforcing business risk
for employers — the more wages are missed, the harder it becomes to operate,
which is exactly the dynamic that has driven Saudi Arabia's wage-compliance
rates above 95 percent in recent years.
