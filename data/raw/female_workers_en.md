# Women in the Workplace — Saudi MHRSD Reference Summary

Saudi Arabia has progressively expanded the rights and protections available
to female workers over the past decade. The following summarizes the
principal protections in force as of 2024.

## Key protections for female workers

### 1. Equal pay and equal treatment

Saudi Labour Law prohibits discrimination in employment on the basis of
gender. Female workers are entitled to:

- Equal pay for work of equal value.
- Equal access to training, promotion, and benefits.
- Equal access to employment contracts, including fixed-term and
  indefinite-term contracts.

### 2. Working hours and overtime

Female workers are subject to the **same working-hour limits as male
workers** (48 hours per week, 8 hours per day). However, additional
restrictions apply:

- **Pregnant and nursing women** may not be required to work overtime, on
  weekly rest days, or on official holidays.
- **Pregnant women** may not be required to work night shifts (the hours
  between sunset and sunrise) under MHRSD regulations.
- Female workers may not be required to work in environments that pose
  specific health risks (e.g. certain chemical exposures) without
  protective measures and explicit consent.

### 3. Maternity leave

A female worker is entitled to **10 weeks (70 days) of fully paid** maternity
leave, distributed at her discretion. The leave may be split, with the
employer being informed at least four weeks in advance.

The employer may not terminate the employment of a female worker during
maternity leave or during illness arising from pregnancy or delivery that
is certified by a medical authority.

### 4. Nursing breaks

Upon return from maternity leave, a nursing mother is entitled to **one
paid hour per day** (or two half-hours) for nursing, until her child
reaches two years of age. The employer must provide an appropriate
location for this purpose.

### 5. Workplace facilities

The employer must provide:

- Separate, sanitary, and adequately equipped rest areas for female
  workers where the work is of a kind that requires such facilities.
- Appropriate childcare or contribution to childcare costs for
  establishments with more than 50 female workers, in line with the
  MHRSD's childcare regulations.

### 6. Transportation

For certain job categories (e.g. industrial work, remote-site work), the
employer must provide safe transportation for female workers to and from
the workplace.

### 7. Dress code and dress standards

The employer may establish a workplace dress code, but it must be
consistent with the general principles of public decency and may not
conflict with the worker's personal or religious obligations.

### 8. Harassment

The employer is required to maintain a written anti-harassment policy and
a confidential internal reporting mechanism. Complaints of workplace
harassment can be filed with the MHRSD, which has the authority to
investigate, impose fines, and in serious cases revoke the employer's
licenses.

### 9. Private-sector jobs

Saudi women now work in a wide range of private-sector jobs that were
previously restricted, including industrial, construction, and security
roles. The MHRSD maintains a list of jobs that are open to women, which
has been progressively expanded.
