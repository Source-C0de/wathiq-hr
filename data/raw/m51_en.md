# Saudi Labour Law — Consolidated Reference (Articles 1–170)

> **Disclaimer:** This document is a plain-language reference summary of the
> Saudi Labour Law (Royal Decree No. M/51, 1426 AH) and its major amendments
> up to 2024. It is provided for educational and informational purposes only

> to the Arabic text published in the Official Gazette and consult a qualified
> Saudi lawyer or the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development
> (MHRSD).

## Chapter 1 — Definitions and General Provisions

**Article 1.** The following terms shall have the meanings assigned to them
below unless the context requires otherwise:

- **Work:** every effort exerted by a worker in return for remuneration, whether
  intellectual, physical, or both.
- **Employer:** any natural or corporate person who employs one or more workers
  in return for remuneration.
- **Worker:** every natural person who works for an employer in return for
  remuneration.
- **Contract:** the employment contract concluded between the employer and the
  worker, which may be concluded for an indefinite period or for a fixed term.
- **Wage:** the basic remuneration paid to the worker in return for his work
  and any allowances agreed upon in the contract, in addition to any other
  benefits in kind.
- **Working hours:** the hours during which the worker is at the disposal of
  the employer, excluding rest periods.

**Article 2.** The provisions of this Law shall apply to:

1. Every contract under which a person works for an employer and under his
   direction in return for remuneration.
2. Workers of the public sector, the military, and domestic workers, to the
   extent not provided for by their special regulations.
3. Skilled workers employed by employers operating in the Free Zones, in
   accordance with the rules agreed upon.

**Article 3.** The provisions of this Law shall not apply to:

1. Members of the employer's family who are his lineal ascendants and
   descendants, spouse, and siblings working in an establishment that employs
   only such family members.
2. Domestic workers and the like, in matters not provided for by this Law.
3. Workers of governmental and public agencies, except for matters relating
   to occupational safety and health.
4. Charitable, social, and cooperative associations and their workers, except
   for matters relating to occupational safety and health.

## Chapter 2 — Employment Contracts

**Article 37.** An employment contract is the agreement concluded between the
employer and the worker, under which the worker undertakes to work for the
employer and under his direction in return for remuneration.

A fixed-term contract is one whose term is fixed by the two parties and ends
upon the lapse of that term. The fixed-term contract shall, upon the expiry of
its term, be deemed renewed for an indefinite period if the two parties
continue to perform their obligations. **The maximum duration of a fixed-term
contract is four years**, renewable by mutual agreement.

If the contract is concluded for an indefinite period, either party may
terminate it for a valid reason by giving the other party notice in writing at
least 30 days prior to the date of termination if the worker is paid monthly,
or 15 days for other workers.

**Article 38.** Where the contract is concluded for an indefinite period, the
worker shall be entitled to an end-of-service benefit computed in accordance
with Article 84 of this Law.

**Article 51.** The worker shall undertake to perform the work agreed upon
under the contract, in a faithful and careful manner, and to comply with the
employer's instructions. The employer shall not require the worker to perform
work that is fundamentally different from the work agreed upon in the contract
except in cases of necessity and for a temporary period.

## Chapter 3 — Probation

**Article 53.** A worker may be appointed on probation for a period not
exceeding **90 days** from the date of commencement of work. The probation
period shall not include Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha holidays and the sick
leave days.

Neither party shall be entitled to wages in lieu of notice during the
probation period. The notice period for either party to terminate the contract
during probation shall be **30 days** unless the contract specifies a longer
period. The worker shall not be placed under probation more than once by the
same employer.

## Chapter 4 — Wages

**Article 94.** All amounts owed to the worker shall be paid in the official
currency of the Kingdom. Wages and any other entitlements shall be paid
**no later than the end of the working day on the first day of the following
month**, unless the contract provides for a shorter interval. Wages shall be
paid directly to the worker or to his bank account.

**Article 95.** Without prejudice to the provisions of Article 94, the employer
may not deduct from the worker's wages except in the following cases:

1. Statutory deductions imposed by law.
2. Amounts owed by the worker to the employer and advanced to him in good
   faith, up to a maximum of 30 percent of the wage.
3. Recovery of amounts paid to the worker in error.
4. Installments of social insurance, loans, or any other amount agreed upon
   in writing between the employer and the worker, provided that the deduction
   does not exceed 25 percent of the wage.

The employer may not transfer the worker's wages to a third party without the
written consent of the worker.

## Chapter 5 — Working Hours and Rest

**Article 98.** The maximum number of regular working hours shall be **48 hours
per week** for all workers. During the month of Ramadan, the working hours of
Muslim workers shall be reduced to **36 hours per week**.

The maximum daily working hours shall not exceed 8 hours, or 6 hours during
Ramadan for Muslim workers, with the exception of cases where shift work is
necessary.

**Article 104.** Every worker shall be entitled to a weekly rest day of **at
least 24 consecutive hours**, normally on Friday. Where work conditions
require, the employer may substitute Friday with another day of the week.

**Article 107.** Overtime is work performed in excess of the normal working
hours. The employer shall pay the worker overtime at the rate of **150 percent
of the basic hourly wage** for regular overtime, and **200 percent of the
basic hourly wage** for work performed on the weekly rest day or on official
holidays.

The total overtime hours shall not exceed 720 hours per year, or the
equivalent of 60 hours per month.

## Chapter 6 — Leaves

**Article 109.** The worker shall be entitled to an annual leave of **21 days**
during the first five years of service, and **30 days** if the worker has
completed five or more years of service. The leave days shall be calculated
on the basis of the actual days of work.

**Article 111.** The employer shall determine the date of commencement of the
annual leave, taking into account the worker's wishes. The employer may
divide the leave into two periods at the request of the worker. The employer
may not deduct from the worker's wages the period of the annual leave.

**Article 112.** Every worker shall be entitled to a fully paid leave on the
following official holidays:

1. Eid al-Fitr: 4 days.
2. Eid al-Adha: 4 days.
3. National Day: 1 day (23 September).

If the holiday falls on a weekly rest day, the worker shall be compensated with
a substitute day.

**Article 115.** Every worker shall be entitled to **Hajj leave for a period of
10 to 15 days**, including the Eid al-Adha holiday, once during the entire
period of service. The leave shall be unpaid if the worker has not yet
completed two years of continuous service.

**Article 117.** The worker shall be entitled to sick leave with pay as
follows:

- First 30 days: full pay.
- Next 60 days: 75 percent of pay.
- Next 30 days: unpaid.

The total sick leave shall not exceed 120 days per year. The employer may
request a medical report to verify the illness.

## Chapter 7 — Occupational Safety and Health

**Article 121.** The employer shall provide a safe and healthy work environment
and shall take all necessary precautions to protect workers from occupational
hazards. The employer shall:

1. Display conspicuous posters of the work safety rules and instructions in
   the workplace.
2. Provide workers with personal protective equipment appropriate to the
   nature of the work, free of charge.
3. Provide first-aid materials and a designated first-aid room.
4. Periodically inspect the workplace to ensure compliance with safety
   requirements.
5. Refrain from allowing workers to perform dangerous tasks without adequate
   training.

## Chapter 8 — Termination of the Contract

**Article 74.** The contract shall not be terminated by the employer without a
valid reason. Where the contract is terminated without a valid reason, the
employer shall be obliged to compensate the worker.

The cases in which the employer may terminate the contract without notice,
award, or indemnity are limited to those specified in Article 80.

**Article 77.** If the contract is unlawfully terminated by the employer, the
worker shall be entitled to compensation equal to the wages for the notice
period, in addition to any other entitlements due under the contract, such as
end-of-service benefits and unused leave.

The court may also award additional compensation to the worker if the
termination was accompanied by circumstances warranting such compensation.

---

## Chapter 9 — End-of-Service Benefits (EOSB)

**Article 84.** A worker who has spent less than five years in continuous
service shall be entitled to end-of-service benefits equal to **half a month's
wage for each year of service**, calculated on the basis of the last wage.

A worker who has spent five or more years in continuous service shall be
entitled to end-of-service benefits equal to **one month's wage for each of
the first five years**, and **half a month's wage for each year thereafter**.

Example calculations:

- **After 5 years:** 5 × (one month's wage) = 5 months' wage.
- **After 10 years:** 5 × (one month's wage) + 5 × (half a month's wage) =
  5 + 2.5 = 7.5 months' wage.

For the purpose of calculating the EOSB, "wage" means the basic wage plus the
housing allowance, if any.

**Article 85.** If the worker resigns before completing five years of
continuous service, he shall be entitled to one-third of the end-of-service
benefit after two years, two-thirds after three to four years, and the full
end-of-service benefit after four or more years.

## Chapter 10 — Non-Compete

**Article 83.** If the worker is engaged in activities that bring him into
contact with the employer's clients or has access to confidential information,
the employer may require the worker to enter into a non-compete agreement
**after the termination of the contract**, provided that:

1. The non-compete is limited in time to **a maximum of two years** from the
   date of termination of the contract.
2. The non-compete is limited in scope to the same type of work.
3. The non-compete is limited geographically to the area in which the
   employer operates.

A non-compete agreement entered into **after the contract has ended** is
permitted only with the worker's written consent and a fair compensation in
return. A non-compete clause added after the contract is already signed (i.e.
during the contract) without new consideration is generally not enforceable.

---

## Chapter 11 — Female Workers

**Article 151.** A female worker shall be entitled to maternity leave of **10
weeks** (70 days) fully paid, distributed at her discretion, starting from the
date of delivery. The leave may be split, with the employer being informed at
least four weeks in advance. The employer may not terminate the employment of
a female worker during her maternity leave or while she is on leave for
illness certified by a medical authority arising from pregnancy or delivery.

Working hours for female workers shall be limited to a maximum of **8 hours
per day**, or 6 hours during Ramadan for Muslim workers. Female workers
shall not be required to work overtime, on weekly rest days, or on official
holidays except in cases of necessity and with their written consent, and in
no case shall pregnant or nursing women be required to perform such work.

Female workers shall be entitled to equal pay and equal treatment in
employment, and the employer shall provide appropriate facilities for nursing
mothers.
